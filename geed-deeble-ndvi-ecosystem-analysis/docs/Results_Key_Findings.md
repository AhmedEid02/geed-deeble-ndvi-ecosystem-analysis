# Results Key Findings

## Geed Deeble Forest Reserve NDVI Analysis

### 1. Overview
The results show a gradual increase in NDVI values in the Geed Deeble Forest Reserve ecosystem between 2000 and 2024. This suggests increased seasonal vegetation greenness during the MAM/Gu season.

However, the increase is mainly associated with expansion of **very sparse vegetation**, not dense forest cover.

### 2. Mean NDVI results

| Year | Mean NDVI |
|---|---:|
| 2000 | 0.113 |
| 2008 | 0.114 |
| 2016 | 0.162 |
| 2024 | 0.201 |

Main finding:

> Mean NDVI increased from **0.113 in 2000** to **0.201 in 2024**.

### 3. NDVI descriptive interpretation
The very small difference between 2000 and 2008 suggests that vegetation greenness remained low during the early period. A stronger increase occurred by 2016, and the highest mean NDVI was observed in 2024.

### 4. NDVI class area results
The NDVI classification shows major change in the proportion of bare/degraded land and sparse vegetation.

#### Bare or degraded land

| Year | Percentage |
|---|---:|
| 2000 | 93.89% |
| 2008 | 91.88% |
| 2016 | 54.80% |
| 2024 | 10.75% |

Main finding:

> Bare/degraded land declined strongly from **93.89% in 2000** to **10.75% in 2024**.

#### Very sparse vegetation

| Year | Percentage |
|---|---:|
| 2000 | 5.07% |
| 2008 | 6.70% |
| 2016 | 39.10% |
| 2024 | 76.19% |

Main finding:

> Very sparse vegetation increased from **5.07% in 2000** to **76.19% in 2024**.

#### Moderate and dense vegetation in 2024

| Class | 2024 percentage |
|---|---:|
| Moderate vegetation | 2.95% |
| Dense vegetation | 0.39% |

Main finding:

> Moderate and dense vegetation remained limited in 2024, indicating that most greening occurred in sparse vegetation classes.

### 5. NDVI change result
The NDVI change map from 2000 to 2024 should be used to identify areas where vegetation greenness increased or decreased.

Interpretation guide:

- Green areas = NDVI increase / vegetation greenness gain
- Red areas = NDVI decrease / vegetation greenness loss
- White or light areas = little or no change

### 6. Main thesis interpretation
> The NDVI analysis indicates that vegetation greenness increased across the Geed Deeble Forest Reserve ecosystem between 2000 and 2024. Mean NDVI rose from 0.113 in 2000 to 0.201 in 2024. The NDVI classification further shows a major decline in bare or degraded land and a strong expansion of very sparse vegetation. However, moderate and dense vegetation remained limited, suggesting that the observed improvement represents dryland seasonal greening or sparse vegetation recovery rather than full forest regeneration.

### 7. Careful discussion points
The student should discuss:

1. The increase in NDVI may reflect rainfall-driven seasonal greening.
2. Expansion of very sparse vegetation does not necessarily mean dense forest recovery.
3. Dryland ecosystems naturally show strong seasonal NDVI variation.
4. Human pressure, grazing, tree cutting, and land-use activities may influence vegetation condition.
5. Rainfall data, field validation, and visual interpretation are recommended to support stronger conclusions.

### 8. What not to overclaim
Do not write:

> Geed Deeble Forest Reserve has fully recovered.

Do not write:

> Human activities are the confirmed cause of vegetation change.

Instead, write:

> The NDVI results suggest increased seasonal vegetation greenness and sparse vegetation expansion, but additional evidence is needed to confirm the drivers of change.

### 9. Suggested results paragraph
> The mean NDVI values showed a gradual increase across the study period. Mean NDVI was 0.113 in 2000 and remained nearly unchanged at 0.114 in 2008. It increased to 0.162 in 2016 and reached 0.201 in 2024. This pattern suggests an overall increase in vegetation greenness during the MAM/Gu season. The NDVI class area results further indicate that bare or degraded land decreased substantially, while very sparse vegetation expanded across the reserve. Despite this positive greening signal, moderate and dense vegetation classes remained limited in 2024, indicating that the reserve was still dominated by sparse vegetation conditions. Therefore, the observed change should be interpreted as dryland greening or sparse vegetation recovery rather than complete forest restoration.
